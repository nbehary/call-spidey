## Traps to expect

1. **Which actor owns the conversation?** `CallConversation` is observed
   by the UI and has an `async` method that awaits a Responder. Under Swift 6
   the compiler will make you say where it runs. Decide, then find out what
   that means for the tests that call it.
2. **Protocol and `Sendable`.** A Responder is handed to the conversation
   and awaited across a suspension. The compiler may ask whether it is safe
   to share. Read the diagnostic before reaching for a fix.
3. **Returning "ignored".** Kotlin's `send` returns `String?`, with `null`
   meaning the Utterance was blank or the Call had ended. Keep that
   contract: M5's turn-taking depends on telling "ignored" from "replied".
4. **Whitespace.** `isBlank()` is not the same as "is empty". Check what
   your Swift test for blankness does with a string of spaces.

## Behaviour to preserve exactly

- The Transcript starts with the Greeting.
- The first Utterance is the Intro: no Responder is consulted, and the
  Reply is "Hi, <name>! What should we talk about?".
- Name extraction: the prefix list and its order, whole-word matching, and
  the fallback name "Pal".
- A blank Utterance is ignored and adds nothing to the Transcript.
- A Reply that ends the Call sets the ended flag, and later Utterances are
  ignored.
- System notes are dropped once the Call has ended.
